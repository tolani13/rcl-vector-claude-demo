Mission and product
Create VECTOR, a single coherent web application for discovering, investigating, comparing, and simulating publicly traded stock opportunities. Initial emphasis: U.S.-tradable penny/microcap stocks, but also compare against small-cap and major-market equities. Research is broad across the world; execution is never real-money in the MVP. The crucial MVP centerpiece is an agent-operated paper-trading laboratory driven by authentic financial, business, macroeconomic, and market observations, not a synthetic market demo. The owner has expressly clarified: the only simulated capital is the seed money. Stock symbols, company records, prices, quotes, volumes, candles, news, filings, analyst commentary, industry events, and catalysts shown as research or market inputs must be real and source-verifiable. Agents follow user-configured strategies, propose paper order actions, have those actions mechanically validated, and monitor/manage paper positions and standing exits. The system must support verifiable historical replay and current observations when real feeds are available. If real data cannot be accessed, do not invent data to keep the screen populated; show a usable NO VERIFIED DATA / DISCONNECTED state with connection/import instructions. A user-supplied file is acceptable only if it contains actual historical records with documented provider, original timestamps, and integrity/quality checks; mere upload does not establish authenticity. Do not mislabel delayed information as live. Virtual cash, paper positions, and estimated paper order executions are simulation artifacts; no paper fill is an actual exchange execution. Mark estimated fills and assumptions distinctly. No live broker order endpoint, real-money order submission, money movement, or automatic real-money execution is permitted in this MVP. The architecture should allow a separately approved future live-broker adapter, not quietly activate it.
Discovery philosophy
Reconnaissance proceeds from (a) macro/political/regulatory/industrial climate, (b) global events and second-/third-order supply-chain consequences, (c) industry decomposition and bottlenecks, (d) companies, products, leaders, contracts, finances, insider behavior and dilution, (e) publication velocity, analyst/commentator views, source dependencies and possible paid promotions, (f) historical breakout and matched non-breakout episodes, and (g) live/replayed tape/price/volume/liquidity. Potential signal: velocity + intent, meaning measured price velocity, acceleration, volume relative to baseline, and where supported actual buy/sell aggressor evidence; volume alone is not 'buying intent'. Explore multiple time horizons independently (minutes vs days/weeks). Record when events occurred, became publicly knowable, were ingested, and were analyzed. No hindsight leakage. Distinguish business quality from trade feasibility and public-event correlation from causation. The known human idea to study historical pre-breakout triggers is core. Keep an explicit 'unknown / insufficient data' state.
